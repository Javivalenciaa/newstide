## 0. How to read this report & data-quality caveats

This document combines four commissioned commands into one audit. Three data caveats affect confidence and are called out inline wherever relevant:

1. **`/seo schema` target was a 404.** The requested URL `https://www.newstide.news/en/article/2026` returns **HTTP 404** — the route `/en/article/[slug]` exists but no article has the slug `2026` (the site uses descriptive slugs, not numeric IDs). Per audit protocol the URL was **not** guessed at; schema analysis was instead run against a valid, representative article — `https://www.newstide.news/en/article/perplexity-overtakes-chatgpt-in-5-key-companies` — plus the homepage and a `/en/fin` article for template consistency. **Action for requester:** re-issue the command with a real article URL if a specific page was intended.
2. **Core Web Vitals are lab/heuristic, not field data.** The Google PageSpeed Insights daily quota (25,000 QPD) was exhausted, and no CrUX field data was retrievable. All LCP/CLS/INP figures are directional estimates derived from direct measurement of live HTML/CSS/JS/image responses. Re-run PSI + CrUX once quota resets to confirm.
3. **Backlink data is Tier 0 (free sources only).** No Moz/Bing/DataForSEO credentials are configured, so no DA/PA, referring-domain counts, anchor text, or toxic-link ratios could be measured. Common Crawl has no record of the domain (expected for a young publisher). No backlink numbers were invented.

Two cross-agent discrepancies were **reconciled** during synthesis (details in the relevant sections):
- **hreflang:** present and correct on EN/ES *article* pairs (confirmed by 3 specialists); the GEO pass could not find it on the *homepage* `<head>` — treat as "implemented on articles, verify hub/home coverage." A separate, real bug exists in the visible language-switcher link (wrong slug).
- **CLS/images:** homepage images correctly use `next/image` (CLS-safe); the **article cover/body images are raw Unsplash `<img>` hotlinks** bypassing the optimizer (LCP + CLS risk). Both statements are true of different templates.

## 1. Executive summary

NewsTide is a technically **well-scaffolded** young publisher undermined by a cluster of **template-level bugs** and a **serious YMYL trust gap**. The foundations are genuinely strong for a site this age: fully server-side-rendered content (ideal for crawlers and AI engines), rich `NewsArticle` / `NewsMediaOrganization` schema, a correctly-built Google News sitemap, self-hosted swap fonts, a real editorial-policy/corrections framework, and a deliberate AI-crawler strategy. Very little here is structural rot — most Critical findings are **single-fix template or pipeline issues** with outsized blast radius.

The problems cluster into four themes:

1. **i18n/template bugs shipped to every page** — duplicated `<title>` suffix on ~328/330 URLs, `<html lang="es">` served on all English URLs, and duplicated/conflicting Organization JSON-LD with contradictory facts. Each is roughly a one-line fix affecting the whole site.
2. **Editorial trust vs. YMYL bar** — personal-finance (money) content is bylined to a single author with no finance credentials, "reviewed by" an entity that doesn't exist as a page or in schema, and cites named authorities (Federal Reserve, CFPB, IRS, BLS) with **zero outbound links**, directly contradicting the site's own published editorial policy. This is the highest-risk area.
3. **Broken/missing surfaces** — 6 dead `/en/fin/category/*` hubs are being submitted to Google as 404s; article pages have a `position:fixed` breadcrumb that overlaps the header; mobile navigation leaves several sections unreachable.
4. **Discoverability & fit** — the site is effectively **absent from organic SERPs** for its own target queries (indexation/authority + a possible "NewsTide" brand-collision issue), and its comparison content is written as narrative news essays where the SERP rewards scannable comparison tables.

**Nothing here blocks the site from succeeding — but the YMYL content issues (Theme 2) carry real penalty/trust risk and should be treated as urgent.**

# COMMAND 2 — `/seo schema` (article) — sub-score 58/100

> ⚠️ **Requested URL `https://www.newstide.news/en/article/2026` = HTTP 404** (route exists, no article with slug `2026`). **Detected on the article:** 3 JSON-LD blocks — a Spanish-locale `WebSite`+`NewsMediaOrganization` graph, an English `WebSite`+`NewsMediaOrganization` graph, and a `NewsArticle` (+ author `Person`, publisher, `ImageObject`, `SpeakableSpecification`, `WebPage`).

### Critical — duplicated, conflicting Organization/WebSite graphs on every page (C5)
Both the Spanish and English `@graph` documents appear on the English page and share the **same `@id`** (`…/#organization`) while carrying **contradictory values** — `foundingDate` "2024" vs "2026", different `publishingPrinciples` URLs, and the English block missing `sameAs`/`masthead`/`knowsAbout`. Two graphs defining one `@id` with conflicting data is invalid per Google guidance and risks arbitrary entity merging/discarding. This is injected by a shared root layout and appears **site-wide** (homepage and `/en/fin` article confirmed). **Root-cause fix:** emit only the active locale's single graph; make `NewsMediaOrganization` one canonical `@id`-referenced entity; **reconcile the real `foundingDate`** (data-integrity bug). A corrected single-graph JSON-LD snippet for `/en/*` was generated.

### Other findings
- **Medium:** no `BreadcrumbList` markup on articles despite a visible breadcrumb (M3) — generated snippet provided.
- **Medium:** `/fin/*` uses `Article`, `/article/*` uses `NewsArticle` (M4) — confirm evergreen-vs-news intent.
- **Low:** author `Person` valid but lacks `@id`/`sameAs`/image (L8); `dateModified` microsecond precision on `/fin` (L6).
- **Info:** `SpeakableSpecification` present but targets `.article-byline` (usually applied to headline/dek — confirm intent); **no `FAQPage` anywhere** — correct given FAQ rich results were retired 2026-05-07 (do **not** add `FAQPage` for SERP benefit).

**The `NewsArticle` block itself is well-formed and rich-result-eligible** — all required + most recommended properties present (headline, ISO dates, author, publisher+logo, 1200×630 image, `mainEntityOfPage`, `inLanguage`, `articleSection`, absolute URLs). The score is held down by the site-wide duplicate-Organization bug and missing breadcrumbs, not the article markup.
